Playbook/Release a new version
Journey · 13 steps in 6 stages

Release a new version

From changes that need a new build to people getting the new version, for an app already in the stores.

The result

A new version of your app, tried on real phones first, has passed the stores' review and is rolling out to people.

Plan your release

Who does what

  • Your moveYou make and save the changes, build and test the new version, and ask to publish.
  • Our team is on itYour ExpoPlatform team approves your request to publish (by default, every request needs their approval), starts the rollout and sets the update prompts.
  • The store is on itApple and Google review each release before people can download it.

Before you start, have ready

  • What you want the stores to show as What's new: App Studio does not send it to either store, so ask your ExpoPlatform contact.
  • Your testers: TestFlight needs at least one, and Google Play testers are Google Groups, where everyone in a group can test.
  • A demo account the store reviewers can sign in with, on a demo event with at least 3 sessions on its agenda.

Plan from the date the new version should reach people

Enter the date the new version should reach people, and the plan works back to the working day each step should be done by. The days are allowances you can change, and your ExpoPlatform team confirms the dates of their own steps. Working days are Monday to Friday: public holidays are not counted.

Change the plan's days

    Where these come from: Apple: App Review (opens in a new tab), read 14 Oct 2026; Google Play Console Help: Publish your app (opens in a new tab), read 14 Oct 2026

    Stage 1 of 6

    Decide what needs a new version

    Most changes you save reach installed apps by themselves, the next time the app refreshes. What is built into the app reaches phones only with a new version.

    1

    Check whether your changes need a new version, or reach installed apps by themselves.

    Your move

    Phones show a change to what is built into the app only after the next build is installed: a test build first, then a store release.

    Done when: You know which of your changes need a new version.

    2

    Make and save every change the new version should carry, before you build.

    Your move

    A build takes the icon and the splash picture and its colour from your app's settings at the time it is made.

    Watch out
    • A home-screen widget's switch saves at once, with no save button, and switching it on or off reaches phones with your next build.
    • In store builds, the name under the icon is the app's store name, not App name in the app: ask your ExpoPlatform contact about the store name.

    Done when: Every change the new version should carry is saved.

    Stage 2 of 6

    Build and test the new version

    Test builds go to your testers with no approval from ExpoPlatform. Each platform takes one release at a time, test and store releases alike.

    3

    In Build & test, press Build for iOS, then Build for Android.

    Your move

    Each build is made from the app code ExpoPlatform deploys, with your app's settings at the time of the build.

    Watch out
    • For Android, keep App Bundle (AAB): it is the file Google Play and its testing need, and the only one Google Play accepts.

    Done when: Each build ends Ready and reads Store-signed, signed for the store so it can be sent to testers; if a ready build is not, ask your ExpoPlatform contact to add the store key that signs your app's builds.

    4

    Under Send to testers, send each new build to your testers, and try it on real phones.

    Your move

    Sending needs no approval from ExpoPlatform, and on TestFlight “Apple checks the build first, usually within a day.”

    Watch out
    • Each platform takes one release at a time, store releases included: while your last version of a platform is still moving, you cannot send a build of that platform.
    • An amber Staged on Play badge means the Android release waits as a draft, not yet out to your testers, until your ExpoPlatform team publishes it in Google Play Console: ask your ExpoPlatform contact.
    • With a demo account saved in Publish, a TestFlight send first checks that its demo event is ready for review.

    Done when: You and your testers have tried the new version: on iPhone, the send shows Live on TestFlight.

    Stage 3 of 6

    Get ready for the stores

    The store listing is your app's page in the stores: one listing for both stores, with its texts in each language, though not all of it reaches each store with a new version. A release at Ready is sent for review on its own, unless Store review still has something to sort out.

    5

    Ask your ExpoPlatform contact what each store will show as What's new for this version.

    Your move

    App Studio does not send your What's new to either store: a Google Play release made from your request carries a standard note, “This update contains performance and stability updates.”

    Watch out
    • Copy, under For the store's “What's new” in a build's Details, only copies the lines: nothing is saved or sent to the stores.

    Done when: You know what each store will show as What's new for this version.

    6

    Under Store review, press Check again, and fix anything marked Your move.

    Your move

    Store reviewers sign in to your app with this account, and a release waits at Ready while Store review has something to sort out.

    Watch out
    • If it says “The demo event looks empty”, pick a demo event with at least 3 sessions on its agenda, or add sessions to it.
    • If it says “The demo event could not be checked”, check the login, the password and the event number, and save them again.

    Done when: Store review reads Ready for review.

    Stage 4 of 6

    Ask to publish

    One request sends both stores their build. ExpoPlatform approves it, and the stores review it.

    7

    On Releases, press Submit to the stores, keep each store's newest build, and press Submit.

    Your move

    Each store with a finished build starts ticked, with its newest build chosen.

    Watch out
    • Let each send to your testers finish first: a request approved while a test send of the same platform is still on its way cannot start.

    Done when: App Studio says “Sent to ExpoPlatform for approval”, or “Sent to the stores” for an app that needs no approval.

    8

    Your ExpoPlatform team approves the request.

    Our team is on it

    When they approve, your store listing is sent, and each build goes on to its store's review.

    Watch out
    • A declined request reads “Declined: [reason]”, or “Declined: no reason given”: sort out what the reason names and submit again, or ask your ExpoPlatform contact when no reason is given.

    Done when: Releases says “Approved [when]: [the builds] went to the stores' review. Follow it below.”

    9

    Apple and Google review the new version; follow it on Releases.

    The store is on it

    Apple typically reviews 90% of submissions in less than 48 hours; for certain developer accounts, Google Play's review can take up to seven days, or longer in exceptional cases. Sources: Apple: App Review (opens in a new tab), read 14 Oct 2026; Google Play Console Help: Publish your app (opens in a new tab), read 14 Oct 2026

    Watch out
    • If a store turns the release down, its card shows the store's message when it gave one, and a new release goes through Submit to the stores and ExpoPlatform's approval again.
    • Cancel release does not take a release out of the store's review: App Studio stops following it, and a review in progress carries on.
    • If your event is close and the iPhone version is still in review, your ExpoPlatform team can ask Apple to expedite the review, and Apple decides. Source: Apple: App Review (opens in a new tab), read 14 Oct 2026

    Done when: The release reads Approved.

    Stage 5 of 6

    Roll it out

    Once a store approves, a later iPhone version, and Android beyond its first 5% stage, wait for your ExpoPlatform team, who start the rollout.

    10

    Your ExpoPlatform team starts the rollout; follow it on Rollout.

    Our team is on it

    Only an app's first iPhone version and Android's first 5% stage go out by themselves: your ExpoPlatform team starts the rollout of later iPhone versions and the rest of Android's.

    Watch out
    • What now says “The rollout starts on its own and reaches people step by step.”, but for a later version that holds only for Android's first 5% stage: ask your ExpoPlatform contact.
    • If What now says “The rollout is stopped”, ExpoPlatform is looking at it: ask your ExpoPlatform contact.

    Done when: The release reads Rolling out, and Live once everyone can get this version.

    11

    On Your apps, check which version people can download from each store.

    Your move

    Under App Store and Google Play, the first badge is what people can download now, and a second badge, when there is one, shows the newest release on its way.

    Watch out
    • A second badge reading Approved means the release passed the store's review and waits for your ExpoPlatform team to start the rollout.

    Done when: The first badge names your new version, such as “Live · 1.94.4”.

    Stage 6 of 6

    Bring people onto the new version

    Your ExpoPlatform team sets whether people on older versions are asked, or made, to update, and Analytics shows which versions phones run.

    12

    Your ExpoPlatform team sets the update rules, if older versions should be asked, or made, to update.

    Our team is on it

    Below the Must update version, people must update before they can use the app; below Asked to update, they are asked and may skip it.

    Watch out
    • These rules are set by your ExpoPlatform team: to change a rule or the wording of a prompt, ask your ExpoPlatform contact.
    • A rule for a version that is not fully live in that store yet waits, and the app is told the rule only once that version is fully live.

    Done when: App updates on your app's Overview shows the rules you asked for.

    13

    In Analytics, open the Devices tab to see which versions phones run.

    Your move

    Versions counts the installs on each version of your app over the last 30 days, each install once, under the version it reported last.

    Done when: Your new version shows under Versions, with its installs.

    Something's wrong?

    Pick what you see.

    • Why: They reach phones only with a new build, and so does switching a widget on or off; in store builds, the name under the icon is the store name, not App name in the app.

      What to do: For the icon, the splash picture and its colour or a widget switch, make a new build, install it and publish it; for the name in store builds, ask your ExpoPlatform contact.

      Go to step 2

    • Why: The app is not in that store yet, there is no Store-signed build of that platform, another release of that platform is still moving (a store release included), or, on Android, an earlier release waits as a draft or the newest build is already on another Google Play track, for example in a store release.

      What to do: Read the reason the dialog gives and do what it says; if new builds are not Store-signed, or a release waits as a draft in Google Play Console, ask your ExpoPlatform contact; while another release is still moving, wait until it has finished.

      Go to step 4

    • Why: The release was created but not sent; with a demo account saved in Publish, its demo event may not be ready for review, or its saved login cannot be used.

      What to do: Check the demo account under Store review in Publish, then press Send again: it continues the same release.

      Go to step 4

    • Why: Store review still has something to sort out, such as a missing demo account, a demo account the check cannot sign in with, or a demo event that looks empty.

      What to do: Open Store review in Publish and fix each item marked Your move; items marked Our team are your ExpoPlatform team's.

      Go to step 6

    • Why: ExpoPlatform approved, but publishing could not start: each platform takes one release at a time, and another release of that platform, such as a test send, was still on its way.

      What to do: Nothing: ExpoPlatform is on it.

      Go to step 8

    • Why: The store found a problem; its card shows the store's message when it gave one.

      What to do: Fix it, build again if needed, and ask again with Submit to the stores: the new release goes through ExpoPlatform's approval and the store's review again.

      Go to step 9

    • Why: Later iPhone versions, and Android beyond its first 5% stage, wait for your ExpoPlatform team to start the rollout.

      What to do: Ask your ExpoPlatform contact.

      Go to step 10

    • Why: The rollout was halted, and ExpoPlatform is looking at it.

      What to do: Ask your ExpoPlatform contact.

      Go to step 10

    • Why: App updates reads Off when no rule is set, and a rule for a version that is not fully live in that store yet waits until it is.

      What to do: Check App updates on your app's Overview, and ask your ExpoPlatform contact to set or change a rule.

      Go to step 12
