Journey · 20 steps in 6 stages

Launch your event app

From a new app to people downloading it from the App Store and Google Play.

The result

Your event's own app is in the App Store and Google Play, with your name, icon, look and content, and you tried it on real phones first.

From the guide: From test build to the stores·Add a new app

Plan from your event date

Who does what

  • Your moveYou build the app in App Studio, test it, write the store listing and ask to publish.
  • Our team is on itYour ExpoPlatform team sets the app up in the App Store and Google Play, approves your request to publish (they do by default) and starts the rollout.
  • The store is on itApple and Google review each release before people can download it.

From the guide: From test build to the stores·Set by your ExpoPlatform team·How to ask ExpoPlatform to publish the app

Before you start, have ready

  • Your event or events in your ExpoPlatform admin. For an app with several events, give them an event tag.
  • The app icon: a PNG or JPEG of up to 10 MB. App Studio advises a square picture of at least 1024 × 1024 with no transparency.
  • Your colours and fonts, and a splash picture if you want one.
  • The store texts for each language: the app name and its descriptions.
  • A demo login for the store reviewers, on a demo event with at least 3 sessions.
  • Your testers: email addresses for TestFlight, Google Group addresses for Google Play.

From the guide: Add a new app·How to change the app icon·Colours & fonts·How to set up the splash screen·How to write the store texts for a language·How to give the store reviewers a demo account·How to send a build to your testers

Plan from your event date

Enter your event date, and the plan works back to the working day each step should be done by. The days are allowances you can change, and your ExpoPlatform team confirms the dates of their own steps. Working days are Monday to Friday: public holidays are not counted.

    Stage 1 of 6

    Start the app

    Create the app first: it starts your ExpoPlatform team's store set-up. While they work on it, do stage 2; the store listing, builds and testers wait until the set-up is done.

    From the guide: How to check what is left before the app can go to the stores·How to build the app for a phone·How to add or remove testers

    1

    Open App Studio from your ExpoPlatform admin.

    Your move

    App Studio opens inside your admin, on Your apps.

    Watch out
    • If App Studio shows “Your session has expired - reopen App Studio from your admin”, open it again from your admin, then check and save again any change you were saving.

    Done when: You see Your apps.

    Go deeper: Open App Studio·Your apps

    2

    Press New app, name the app, choose its events and press Create.

    Your move

    “It starts as a draft: nothing reaches the stores until you build and publish it.”

    Watch out
    • Several events works by event tag: if no event has one, tag your events in your admin, or choose One event.
    • Start from can copy the look and home-screen widget settings of an app you already have.

    Done when: The new app opens on its Overview.

    Go deeper: Add a new app·Events & updates

    3

    Your ExpoPlatform team sets the app up in the App Store and Google Play.

    Our team is on it

    Creating the app sends them a set-up request; they approve it and choose the store account the app is published under.

    Watch out
    • Until it is done, both build buttons are greyed out: wait, and ask your ExpoPlatform contact if you need to know where it stands.
    • If they decline, App Studio says “ExpoPlatform did not set this app up in the stores”: ask your ExpoPlatform contact.

    Done when: Publish shows its tabs: Overview, Store listing, Releases and Rollout.

    Go deeper: From test build to the stores·How to check what is left before the app can go to the stores·Add a new app·Set by your ExpoPlatform team·How to build the app for a phone

    Stage 2 of 6

    Make it your event's app

    You can do all of this while the store set-up runs. Settle the icon and the splash picture early: they reach phones only with a new build. Sign-in, the menu and the look each have a journey of their own, linked from their step.

    From the guide: How to check what is left before the app can go to the stores·How to build the app for a phone·How to add or remove testers·When your changes reach the app

    4

    Set the app icon and the splash in Name, icon & splash, and press Save changes.

    Your move

    The icon, the name under it, and the splash picture and its colour are built into each build of the app.

    Watch out
    • The save bar says changes reach the app at the next refresh, but phones show these only once a new build is installed.
    • In store builds, the name under the icon is the app's store name, not App name in the app.

    Done when: Your icon shows in Name, icon & splash after you save.

    8

    In Home screen, switch Designed home screen on, then design each event's home screen.

    Your move

    Colours, the home screen, the menu and pages reach installed apps at their next refresh: you can keep improving them after launch, with no new build.

    Watch out
    • A new app starts with Designed home screen off, unless it was copied from an app where it is on.

    Done when: Designed home screen is on, the phone preview shows the home screen you want, and you saved it.

    Stage 3 of 6

    Try it on real phones

    Test builds go to your testers with no approval from ExpoPlatform.

    From the guide: How to send a build to your testers

    9

    Open Build & test and press Build for iOS, then Build for Android.

    Your move

    You can leave the page while a build runs, and an iOS and an Android build can run at the same time.

    Watch out
    • For Android, keep App Bundle (AAB): Google Play accepts only an App Bundle.
    • If a build runs for a long time, an amber note says it “may have stopped without telling us”: stop it and start a new one.

    Done when: Each build ends Ready and reads Store-signed; if a ready build is not Store-signed, ask your ExpoPlatform contact to add your app's store key.

    Go deeper: How to build the app for a phone·How to follow a build, and stop it·How to install a test build on your phone·How to send a build to your testers

    10

    Under Send to testers, send each build to TestFlight (iPhone) or Google Play internal testing (Android).

    Your move

    Apple checks a TestFlight build first, “usually within a day”, while Google Play internal testing is ready “within minutes, with no review”.

    Watch out
    • Each platform takes one release at a time, store releases included: wait for one send to finish before the next.
    • An amber Staged on Play badge means the Android release waits as a draft in Google Play Console until it is published there: ask your ExpoPlatform contact.

    Done when: Your testers have the app: on iPhone, the send shows Live on TestFlight.

    Go deeper: How to send a build to your testers·How to add or remove testers·From test build to the stores

    11

    Install the test build from TestFlight or Google Play, or from its install link, and try every screen.

    Your move

    App Studio's own advice: “Try the build on real phones before it goes to the store.”

    Done when: You and your testers are happy with the app.

    Go deeper: How to install a test build on your phone·How to send a build to your testers·How to ask ExpoPlatform to publish the app·Build & test

    Stage 4 of 6

    Prepare the store listing

    Your store listing is your app's page in both stores. Nothing you save here goes to the stores until ExpoPlatform approves your request to publish.

    From the guide: From test build to the stores·Publish

    12

    In Publish, on Store listing, write the texts for each language and save each one.

    Your move
    Watch out
    • A screenshot capture takes one pass per language of your store listing, or English when it has none: add your languages first.
    • Google Play does not get these texts from App Studio, and neither store gets What's new: ask your ExpoPlatform contact how your texts reach Google Play.

    Done when: Every language you need is saved.

    Go deeper: How to write the store texts for a language·Set by your ExpoPlatform team·From test build to the stores·How to take store screenshots of your app

    13

    In Store screenshots, take the screenshots, make store artwork in Store studio, and attach it to your listing.

    Your move
    Watch out
    • When the app has no screenshot build for that store yet, Take screenshots builds the app first, so it too waits for the store set-up.
    • Your Store studio set is held only in the page: attach it before you leave or reload the page.

    Done when: See them in Publish shows the pictures on your listing.

    Go deeper: How to take store screenshots of your app·How to turn your screenshots into store artwork·How to put the set on your store listing

    14

    Under Store review, give the store reviewers a demo account, and press Save demo account.

    Your move

    The store reviewers sign in to your app with it, and it is the only set-up step that is yours.

    Watch out
    • Without it, the iPhone submission fails and the release stays Ready.
    • Pick a demo event with real sessions and exhibitors: the review check looks for at least 3 sessions on its agenda.
    • Notes for the reviewers are kept in App Studio, but not sent with your submissions.

    Done when: App Studio says “Demo account saved”.

    Go deeper: How to give the store reviewers a demo account·Publish·From test build to the stores

    15

    Open Publish and read each store's card and What now.

    Your move

    Each store card shows six steps (Set up, Build, Test, Store listing, Publish and Live) and who acts next: Your move, Our team is on it or The store is on it.

    Done when: Set up, Build and Test read Done on both store cards, and Store listing reads Done or, before the app's first release, What now says “Your listing goes to [store] with your publication”.

    Go deeper: How to check what is left before the app can go to the stores·From test build to the stores

    Stage 5 of 6

    Go to the stores

    One request sends both stores their build. ExpoPlatform approves it, the stores review it, and the app rolls out.

    From the guide: From test build to the stores

    16

    On Releases, press Submit to the stores, keep each store's newest build, and press Submit.

    Your move
    Watch out
    • One request can wait at a time: Submit to the stores is greyed out until ExpoPlatform decides.

    Done when: App Studio says “Sent to ExpoPlatform for approval”, or “Sent to the stores” for an app that needs no approval.

    Go deeper: How to ask ExpoPlatform to publish the app·From test build to the stores

    17

    Your ExpoPlatform team approves the request.

    Our team is on it

    When they approve, your store listing is sent, and each build goes on to its store's review.

    Watch out
    • A declined request reads “Declined: [reason]”, or “Declined: no reason given”: sort out what the reason names and submit again, or ask your ExpoPlatform contact when no reason is given.

    Done when: Releases says “Approved [when]: [the builds] went to the stores' review. Follow it below.”

    Go deeper: From test build to the stores·How to ask ExpoPlatform to publish the app·How to write the store texts for a language·Set by your ExpoPlatform team

    18

    Apple and Google review the release. Follow it on Releases.

    The store is on it

    Apple typically reviews 90% of submissions in less than 48 hours; for certain developer accounts, Google Play's review can take up to seven days, or longer in exceptional cases. Sources: Apple: App Review , read 14 Oct 2026; Google Play Console Help: Publish your app , read 14 Oct 2026

    Watch out
    • If a store turns the release down, its card shows the store's message when it gave one, and a new release goes through Submit to the stores and ExpoPlatform's approval again.
    • If your event is close and the iPhone version is still in review, your ExpoPlatform team can ask Apple to expedite the review, and Apple decides. Source: Apple: App Review , read 14 Oct 2026

    Done when: The release reads Approved.

    Go deeper: How to follow a release through the store's review·From test build to the stores·Publish·Set by your ExpoPlatform team

    19

    The app rolls out to people.

    Our team is on it

    Apple releases an app's first iPhone version on approval, and Android's first 5% stage goes out by itself; the rest waits for your ExpoPlatform team, who start the rollout.

    Watch out
    • The Overview can still read Not in the stores yet when people can already download the app: Your apps shows the live version.

    Done when: The release reads Live.

    Go deeper: How to follow a rollout·From test build to the stores·Your app's Overview

    Something's wrong?

    Pick what you see.

    • Why: ExpoPlatform has not finished setting the app up in the stores, or did not set it up.

      What to do: Read the note above the list: while the set-up runs, wait, and ask your ExpoPlatform contact if you need to know where it stands; if it says “ExpoPlatform did not set this app up in the stores”, ask your ExpoPlatform contact.

      Go to step 3 ·How to build the app for a phone

    • Why: The store set-up is not finished.

      What to do: Read the line under it: while your request waits, wait until the set-up is done; once ExpoPlatform is putting your app in the stores, ask your ExpoPlatform contact if this takes longer than a working day; if they did not set it up, ask your ExpoPlatform contact.

      Go to step 3 ·How to check what is left before the app can go to the stores

    • Why: They reach phones only with a new build, and so does switching a widget on or off; in store builds, the name under the icon is the store name, not App name in the app.

      What to do: For the icon, the splash picture and its colour or a widget switch, make a new build, install it and publish it; for the name in store builds, ask your ExpoPlatform contact.

      Go to step 4 ·When your changes reach the app·How to change the app's name in the app·Update your app after launch·Home-screen widgets

    • Why: Designed home screen is off (“When off, the app opens on its first menu screen and this design is not used.”), or the Preview strip below the form starts the preview on My Badge or Boot flow.

      What to do: Switch Designed home screen on and press Save home screen, or choose Home in the Preview strip.

      Go to step 8 ·How to turn the designed home screen on or off·Find your way around

    • Why: It may have stopped: an amber note on its card says so.

      What to do: Stop it and start a new one. If your builds keep failing, ask your ExpoPlatform contact.

      Go to step 9 ·How to follow a build, and stop it

    • Why: The app is not in that store yet, there is no Store-signed build of that platform, another release of that platform is still moving (a store release included), or, on Android, an earlier release waits as a draft or the newest build is already on another Google Play track.

      What to do: Read the reason the dialog gives and do what it says; if new builds are not Store-signed, or a release waits as a draft in Google Play Console, ask your ExpoPlatform contact; while another release is still moving, wait until it has finished.

      Go to step 10 ·How to send a build to your testers

    • Why: A request is already waiting for ExpoPlatform.

      What to do: Wait for their decision; it shows on Releases.

      Go to step 16 ·How to ask ExpoPlatform to publish the app

    • Why: Store review still has something to sort out, such as a missing demo account, a demo account the check cannot sign in with, or a demo event that looks empty.

      What to do: Open Store review in Publish and fix each item marked Your move; items marked Our team are your ExpoPlatform team's.

      Go to step 14 ·From test build to the stores·How to give the store reviewers a demo account

    • Why: The store found a problem; its card shows the store's message when it gave one.

      What to do: Fix it, build again if needed, and ask again with Submit to the stores: the new release goes through ExpoPlatform's approval and the store's review again.

      Go to step 18 ·From test build to the stores·How to follow a release through the store's review

    • Why: The Overview counts a store as live only from App Studio's own store record.

      What to do: Check the live version on Your apps.

      Go to step 19 ·Your app's Overview
